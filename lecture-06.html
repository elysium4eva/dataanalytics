What the chapter covers

How marketing data become a number a manager can act on: conjoint design and part-worths, discrete choice and the IIA problem, hedonic regression, and response models that allocate a budget.

Outline

Part 1Survey design (5–13)
What survey methodology is, inference and representativeness, the anatomy of a survey, four kinds of question, survey error and cost, seven methodological decisions, modes and mode effects, the design checklist
Part 2Conjoint analysis (14–23)
What conjoint does, part-worths and market models, the four design steps — study type, attributes, levels, questionnaire — a worked laptop example, reading part-worths, advantages and disadvantages
Part 3Choice models (24–37)
Discrete against continuous choice, random utility and McFadden, individual and aggregate data, an NEV policy example, binomial and multinomial logit, the choice set, choice probabilities, the IIA problem and the models that relax it
Part 4Hedonic regression (38–42)
Real-estate valuation, the hedonic equation, where the data come from, and applications beyond property
Part 5Response models (43–48)
What a response model is, three questions it answers, four insights, the concave response curve, budget allocation from response models, and how to read all of this critically

Learning objectives

  • Design a conjoint study: choose the type, the attributes, the levels and the questionnaire format;
  • Estimate and read part-worths, and turn them into share-of-preference statements;
  • Write down a logit model from random utility, and explain what IIA assumes and how nested or mixed logit relaxes it;
  • Specify a hedonic regression and say what its coefficients do and do not mean;
  • Derive marginal return from a response curve and allocate a budget across channels;
  • Read a vendor's marketing-analytics claim critically: what was the design, the sample and the counterfactual.
