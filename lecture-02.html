What the chapter covers

Where data come from, how they are measured, and what it costs to get them: measurement levels, source scoring, APIs, scraping, documents, streaming, surveys, experiments and governance.

Outline

§1Data sourcing overview
The landscape of sources, a five-dimension scoring grid, the error chain, ten questions to ask of any data set
§2Types, structures and measurement
From the world to a variable, levels of measurement, numerical and categorical data with worked samples, structured / semi-structured / unstructured, text, image, audio, video, graph and spatio-temporal data
§3Data sources, international and China
In-house and proprietary data, international open data, Chinese government statistics, DaaS providers, CSMAR against Wind, dataset platforms, REST APIs with runnable skeletons
§4Unstructured and streaming
Web scraping and its legal boundaries, PDF and scanned documents, table normalisation, LLM-based structured extraction, streaming data, alternative data and its compliance
§5Primary data collection
When you must collect it yourself, questionnaire design in three parts, sampling methods, modes and the cost–coverage trade-off, nonresponse and weighting, census and administrative data, experiments and A/B tests, interviews, card sorting, crowdsourcing
§6Quality control and LLM assistance
Data health checks, LLM-assisted collection and its verification workflow, synthetic data and privacy-enhancing techniques, the primary-data quality checklist
§7Pipeline, governance and compliance
ETL against ELT, a minimal viable pipeline, data contracts and data products, the modern stack, metadata and lineage, versions and reproducibility, quality rules, GDPR/PIPL, copyright and licences
§8Workflow, assignment and close
Translating a research question into a data request, the source inventory template, assignment 1, common pitfalls, cheat sheets

Learning objectives

  • Classify any variable by measurement level and data structure, and name the analysis that level permits;
  • Score a data source on coverage, granularity, reliability, cost and licence before you download it;
  • Pull data from an API, scrape a public page legally, and extract tables from documents reproducibly;
  • Design a questionnaire and a sampling plan that will not embarrass you at the analysis stage;
  • Run and read an A/B test, including the traps: peeking, multiple comparisons, interference;
  • Write the governance layer — contracts, lineage, versions, privacy — that makes a data set usable by others.
