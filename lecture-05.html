What the chapter covers

The chapter that makes the rest of the analytics sequence usable: four paradigms, the split-first discipline, bias–variance, metrics and calibration, the algorithm families, networks, and how to ship a model honestly.

Outline

Part 1What machine learning is (7–18)
Three opening questions, Mitchell's definition, the AI/ML/DL/generative map, applications in finance and business, three data shapes, supervised against unsupervised
Part 2Workflow, splitting and evaluation (19–30)
Prediction against causation, machine learning against econometrics, what happens when $P>N$, the three splits, cross-validation, a pipeline and a baseline, four protocols and four scores
Part 3Generalisation and evaluation (31–46)
Overfitting and underfitting, three hypotheses on one data set, bias–variance and the three sources of error, learning curves, regularisation paths, regression and classification metrics, cost-sensitive thresholds, calibration
Part 4Supervised algorithms (47–61)
The family map, linear and penalised regression, k-NN, SVM, trees and ensembles, gradient boosting implementations, naive Bayes and LDA, the estimator decision table
Part 5Unsupervised algorithms (62–73)
What unsupervised learning is for, PCA and factor analysis, non-linear embeddings, four clustering families, k-means and its misuses, hierarchy, density and mixtures, evaluation, anomalies and association rules
Part 6Networks and reinforcement (74–87)
From regression to networks, the mechanics of one neuron, architecture as hyper-parameters, forward and backward propagation, training loops, deep nets, reinforcement learning and Q-learning, transfer and fine-tuning, build or reuse in 2026
Part 7Trustworthy delivery (88–99)
Three families of explainability, permutation importance, PDP and ICE, the leakage checklist, fairness, the model card, MLOps, AutoML, cost and latency, metric decay, compliance
Part 8Tools, project and close (100–104)
The tool stack, the one-page project statement, the top ten mistakes, glossary and references

Learning objectives

  • Separate supervised, unsupervised, self-supervised and reinforcement learning, and say what data each needs;
  • Explain over- and underfitting with bias–variance and the three sources of error, and read a learning curve;
  • Run a correct train / validation / test split and cross-validation, and recognise the six kinds of leakage;
  • Choose and evaluate a baseline: regression and classification metrics, thresholds against business cost, calibration;
  • State the mechanics of a neural network and of reinforcement learning — forward pass, activation, backpropagation, reward;
  • Deliver a trustworthy model: interpretability, a model card, a leakage checklist and a one-page project statement.
